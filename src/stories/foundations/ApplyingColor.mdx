# Applying color

Color in Inkwell reinforces hierarchy and meaning. It rarely decorates.

## The three-layer stack

Inkwell frames every surface as one of three layers. Together they create a
sense of depth without needing explicit elevation or shadows — think of them
as a **z-axis for the screen**, ordered from behind to in front.

### Canvas — the page behind everything

`--color-bg-canvas` is the base color for the app. Panels and cards sit on top
of it. Text should meet AA contrast when placed directly on the canvas — most
prose UI belongs on a surface instead.

### Surface — the raised content plane

Surfaces are where content lives — cards, panels, tables, modals. They read as
lighter than the canvas, which is what creates the sense of layering.

| Token | When to use |
|---|---|
| `--color-surface-default` | Main content surfaces (Panel, Card, table body). |
| `--color-surface-raised` | Content that sits *on top of* a surface — popover, tooltip, modal card. |
| `--color-surface-active` | The "selected" or currently-active state of a surface item. |

### Overlay — the interaction fills

Overlays are subtle color washes applied *on top of* surfaces to indicate
interaction (hover, focus, selection). They use white with low alpha so they
work over any surface.

| Token | Alpha | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-overlay-soft` | ~4% | Hover fills, input backgrounds, subtle selections. |
| `--color-overlay-strong` | ~7% | Active/pressed states, stronger selections. |

## Interaction states

Interaction states follow a consistent recipe across components: **the surface
gets a hover overlay, the border brightens, and focus adds a ring.**

| State | Surface | Border | Ring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest | `--color-surface-*` | `--line-soft` | — |
| Hover | + `--color-overlay-soft` | `--line` (brighter) | — |
| Focus | (unchanged) | `--color-focus-ring` | `0 0 0 2px var(--color-focus-outline)` |
| Active | + `--color-overlay-strong` | `--line` | — |
| Disabled | (unchanged) | `--line-soft` | — (opacity ~ 0.5) |

The [Button](?path=/docs/components-button--docs) and
[Avatar](?path=/docs/components-avatar--docs) show this pattern in practice.

## Semantic roles

Beyond neutral surfaces, color carries meaning. Every semantic role has a
paired *foreground* token (for text and icons) and a *soft* variant (for muted
backgrounds — Pill, StatusBanner).

| Role | Foreground | Soft background | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accent (brand) | `--accent` | — | Primary action, brand emphasis. Sparingly. |
| Success | `--color-success` | `--color-success-soft` | Confirmation, saved state, positive outcome. |
| Warning | `--color-warning` | `--color-warning-soft` | Non-blocking caution, "check this before continuing." |
| Danger | `--danger` | `--color-danger-soft` | Destructive actions, errors, blocking problems. |
| Muted | `--muted` | — | Secondary text — timestamps, hints, non-essential meta. |

## General semantic usage

- **Contrast, always.** Text on any surface must meet WCAG AA. When in doubt, use `--text` on any surface token and `--muted` for secondary text.
- **Never lean on color alone.** Danger and success states also need an icon, label, or shape — some users can't distinguish red from green.
- **Semantic tokens for semantics.** Don't reach for `--accent` to mean "success" just because both are recognizable; each has a specific meaning.
- **Hover is a wash, not a re-skin.** Interaction states brighten what's there. They don't swap the base color.
